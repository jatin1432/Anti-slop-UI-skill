# Direction: deciding what this page should feel like

Read this at stage 2, after intake. The output is a six-line plan, checked against the generic defaults before any code is written.

## Where a direction comes from

Distinctive choices come from the subject, not from a style list. Before picking anything, name the subject's own world: its materials, its vocabulary, the places it lives, the objects its people handle, the era it belongs to. A climbing-gear brand, a children's reading app and a bond-trading terminal should not be able to swap palettes.

Useful sources of decisions:

- **Materials and objects** in the subject (paper, steel, fabric, glass, soil, screens).
- **The use scene:** who looks at this, where, in what light, for how long. This decides light or dark far better than the category does. A night-shift tool, a cinema brand or a music app may want dark; a recipe site read in a kitchen at noon probably does not. Never default to either.
- **What the brand already owns:** a logo colour, a typeface, a photograph style, a tone of voice.
- **The one thing a visitor should remember** (the signature, below).

## The six-line plan

Write it in the reply or the design note. Every line must be a concrete decision; a line that can only be filled with adjectives marks the part of the design that is not yet thought through.

1. **Who and what for:** the person, their device, what they came to do. Pick one main viewport.
2. **The lead and the action:** on the first screen and on each key state, the one thing that leads and the one action that matters. Say how the other actions step back.
3. **Convention and departure:** the layout people already expect for this kind of page (keep it), and the one place this page breaks from it, with a reason drawn from the product. No reason, no departure.
4. **Direction in a sentence:** the first impression and what the page is organised around.
5. **Type, colour and the main picture:** named typefaces and their roles; background, text, accent and the colours derived from them, and where each comes from in the subject; the hero image or graphic and its source.
6. **Motion and states:** how the main action responds, one state change, the first-load moment; and the states the main flow passes through (empty, loading, error, success).

Words like "premium", "clean", "modern" or "elegant" are not decisions. Replace each with something visible, such as a type size, a colour, an amount of space, or a photograph.

When the person should confirm the direction, send lines 2 to 5 compressed into a few lines and stop. When they said "go ahead" or nobody is there to answer, continue.

## The signature

Every Sell or Show page needs a single memorable element: the strongest real asset the subject has, presented as well as it can be. Examples: a hotel's best photograph of its view, the app performing its core task live, an unbeatable price set at poster size, an interactive control the visitor can play with. It sits on the first screen. Everything else is quieter so it can lead.

A number drawn as a chart, a gradient blob or an abstract 3D shape is decoration, not a signature.

## Three dials

Set each from 1 to 10 for the surface and write them in the plan. They keep the direction honest.

- **Variance:** 1 is centred, symmetrical and conventional; 10 is asymmetric, experimental and editorial.
- **Motion:** 1 is feedback only; 10 is choreographed, scroll-driven storytelling.
- **Density:** 1 is airy with very large spacing; 10 is a packed working dashboard.

Typical settings: Sell pages 5 to 8 variance, 3 to 7 motion, 2 to 4 density. Work screens 2 to 4 variance, 1 to 3 motion, 6 to 9 density.

## Check the plan against the generic defaults

Generated pages cluster around a handful of complete looks. Each is legitimate when the brief asks for it; each is a failure when it shows up because nothing was decided.

- Warm off-white background, high-contrast serif headline, a single terracotta or clay accent.
- Near-black background with one acid green, electric blue or bright orange accent.
- Newspaper pastiche: hairline rules, zero radius, dense columns, small caps everywhere.
- The card kit: every section chopped into identical rounded cards with the same soft shadow and a gradient wash.
- Template furniture on any subject: tracked capital labels over every heading, dot-separated meta lines, arrows glued to every link, a monospace font for small labels, a pulsing "live" dot.

Then run the **mirror test**: would a similar request in the same category ("a landing page for another headphone brand") produce this exact plan? If yes, change at least one major decision (layout, type, colour or the hero) and say which and why.

Also run the **brief test**: does every line trace to something in the brief, the subject or the material inventory? Rewrite any line that does not.

## Hero layouts to choose from

These are starting points for the first screen of a Sell or Show page. Pick by the material available; never use the same layout twice in a row for one person; adapt each one to the subject rather than copying it.

| Layout | What it is | Needs | Suits |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Product over type** | The headline set very large across the full width, the product photographed as a clean cutout and placed over it, so image and type overlap in depth | A good cutout of a physical product | Hardware, consumer goods, launches |
| **Wordmark across a scene** | A full-bleed photograph with the brand name or headline running across it at huge size, possibly scrolling sideways; tiny labels pinned in the corners | One strong atmospheric photograph | Brands, places, events, energy and outdoor |
| **Collage and condensed type** | Several cropped photographs in shaped frames on one side, very large condensed capitals on the other | Several real photos of people or product | Fashion, fragrance, culture, agencies |
| **Annotated picture** | A wide photo or render with thin callout lines pointing at its parts, small technical labels, headlines placed on either side | An image with parts worth naming | Engineering, architecture, outdoor gear, science |
| **Cinematic scene** | A dark or moody full-screen scene, the name set at the bottom edge, a small info card in a corner that opens into the story on scroll | A dramatic image or illustration | Games, film, music, storytelling brands |
| **Work gallery strip** | A centred headline above a curved or angled strip of real work samples that runs off both edges | Ten or more real pieces of work | Studios, portfolios, template and design tools |
| **Live product** | The real product interface, built or captured, doing its one job in the hero, with the headline beside it | Access to the product or screenshots | Software, apps, developer tools |
| **Type only** | No picture at all: one very large, carefully set statement, a short paragraph, one action, with the space doing the work | A strong, short line of copy | Manifestos, studios, services with no imagery |

Light or dark is never part of the layout. Choose it from the use scene and the brand.

## Hero techniques that make it feel designed

Apply the ones the material and mode support, not all of them.

1. **Monumental type.** Set the headline or name far larger than default, close to edge to edge, in one confident face. Size and weight carry emphasis, not colour tricks.
2. **Layer in depth.** Let the main image overlap the headline (image in front of type, or type in front of image) so the hero reads as a composition, not as boxes placed side by side.
3. **One light, real shadows.** Light the product or scene from one clear direction and give it a soft shadow that grounds it. Glow that comes from nowhere looks generated.
4. **A strict grid with generous empty space.** Let space carry the layout. Pin small facts (specs, price, location, year) to the corners or to grid lines rather than putting them in cards.
5. **Gather the controls.** One compact control group (for example a small floating bar holding the price and the main action) instead of buttons scattered around the hero.
6. **One living moment.** A single motion in the hero (the product turning, the name drifting, a photo slowly breathing) while everything else stays still.

## Direction for Work and Read surfaces

Dashboards, tools and docs get their identity from precision, not spectacle: a well-chosen typeface, a tight and consistent spacing scale, one accent colour used only for meaning, excellent tables and forms, and states that are clearly designed. The first screen is the work itself: compact header, first row of content fully visible, no slogans or explanatory paragraphs above it.
